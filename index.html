Professor of Mathematics

Taylan Şengül

Department of Mathematics · Marmara University

I am an applied mathematician working on nonlinear dynamical systems and partial differential equations. My research focuses on dynamic transitions, bifurcation and pattern formation, with applications to classical and geophysical fluid dynamics.

Research

When a nonlinear system loses stability, linear theory says only that it does — not what happens next. My work is about what happens next: whether the loss of stability is continuous or catastrophic, which pattern the system selects, and whether the new state is unique. The tool is dynamic transition theory: reduce the dynamics to the centre manifold spanned by the critical modes, then read the transition type off a small number of coefficients that can, in the right coordinates, be computed in closed form.

Transition numbers in closed form

Most of my recent work pushes the reduction far enough that the transition coefficient becomes an explicit formula in the parameters of the equation, rather than a number left to numerics. This has been carried out for 1D reaction–diffusion equations in the self-adjoint and non-self-adjoint cases, for higher-order nonlinearities, and for nonlinearities carrying derivatives of arbitrary order.

Symmetry, mode interaction and pattern selection

Symmetry forces eigenvalues to cross in groups, so the interesting transitions are rarely simple. I study what the resulting degenerate crossings select — interactions of neighbouring modes, hexagonal patterns, rolls and rectangles in Rayleigh–Bénard convection — and, currently, Hopf bifurcation under O(2) symmetry, where the nonlinearity's structure can force the bifurcation direction to degenerate.

Geophysical and classical fluids

The same machinery applied where the transition has a physical name: multiple equilibria of the thermohaline circulation, zonal flows in a two-layer quasi-geostrophic ocean, double Hopf bifurcation of western boundary currents, and instabilities of non-Newtonian Poiseuille flow and the viscous Burgers equation.

Group and service

I supervise a small group working on dynamic transitions and their reductions. Active lines:

  • PhD Transitions in delay systems · degenerate transition numbers, where the leading transition coefficient vanishes
  • MSc Transitions with drift on a periodic ring · the degenerate Bautin locus of a Robin-type boundary problem
  • Undergraduate Symbolic computation of the bifurcation direction for the FitzHugh–Nagumo equation with Robin boundary conditions
  • Postdoc Modulation and long-interval asymptotics of dynamic transitions

Prospective students: I have no funded position open, but I keep a bank of well-posed problems and am glad to discuss one. Write with a short note on what you have read. I serve as an Associate Editor of Mathematical Methods in the Applied Sciences (since 2021).
